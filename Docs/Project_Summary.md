# Project Summary: Human TLR4 In Silico Analysis

## Overview
This project presents an integrated in-silico analysis of the human **Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4)**, a pattern-recognition receptor involved in innate immune responses and the recognition of microbial components. The study combines sequence analysis, protein characterization, structural investigation, functional enrichment, genetic variant assessment, and cancer-related expression and survival analysis to explore the biological characteristics and potential clinical relevance of TLR4.

## Objectives
- Characterize the human TLR4 gene and protein using public biological databases.
- Investigate sequence features, physicochemical properties, and evolutionary conservation.
- Examine protein structure, secondary structural elements, and predicted membrane topology.
- Explore TLR4-associated protein–protein interactions and enriched biological pathways.
- Assess the clinical significance of reported genomic variants.
- Investigate TLR4 expression patterns across cancer types and evaluate its association with overall survival.

## Methodology and Tools
The analysis utilized resources including **NCBI, Ensembl, UniProtKB, Clustal Omega, Jalview, RCSB PDB, DSSP, PyMOL, DeepTMHMM, STRING, and GEPIA**, alongside functional enrichment and variant annotation resources. Multiple sequence alignment was used to investigate conservation, structural tools supported protein visualization and characterization, and enrichment analysis explored biological processes and pathways associated with TLR4. Pan-cancer expression and Kaplan–Meier survival analyses were conducted to examine potential associations between TLR4 expression and patient outcomes.

## Key Areas of Investigation
The project integrates genomic and protein-level characterization with functional, structural, and clinical data. Survival analysis identified statistically significant associations between TLR4 expression groups and overall survival in five cancer types: **KIRC, LAML, LUAD, THYM, and TGCT** (p < 0.05 in the respective analyses). These findings indicate potential cancer-specific associations that require further investigation and independent validation.

## Significance
This project demonstrates how publicly available bioinformatics resources can be combined to investigate a gene of immunological importance across multiple biological levels. It provides a foundation for understanding TLR4 sequence and structural characteristics, immune-related functional associations, genetic variation, and potential relevance to cancer biology.

**Project focus:** Computational biology | Immunoinformatics | Molecular biology | Protein structure analysis | Functional enrichment | Pan-cancer analysis

**Note:** The findings are computational and exploratory; they do not independently establish causality, clinical utility, or experimental validation.
